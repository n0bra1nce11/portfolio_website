## 2.0 Methodology

Testing follows two named, industry-standard frameworks rather than an ad hoc checklist:

- **OWASP Web Security Testing Guide (WSTG)** for application-layer coverage — input validation,
  authentication, session management, access control, and business logic testing.
- **PTES (Penetration Testing Execution Standard)** for the overall engagement structure —
  pre-engagement, intelligence gathering, threat modeling, exploitation, post-exploitation, and
  reporting.

Findings are then re-mapped from a testing-framework taxonomy onto an audit-framework taxonomy
(ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A) in [Section 5.0](#5-0-control-mapping), because a GRC audience needs
"which control failed," not just "which OWASP category."

## 3.0 Threat model

A hospital management system's primary asset is **PHI (protected health information)** — patient
identity, diagnosis, treatment, and billing records — not just uptime or generic CRUD data. The
threat model below is what actually shaped test case selection.

**Assets**
- Patient records (identity + clinical data)
- Appointment and scheduling data (can leak patient presence/attendance patterns)
- Staff/admin credentials and role assignments
- Audit logs themselves (a tampered log is worse than no log)

**Primary threats**
- Horizontal privilege escalation between patient accounts (one patient reading another's records)
- Vertical privilege escalation from patient/receptionist role to clinician/admin role
- Insufficient audit logging of PHI access (undermines accountability and breach detection)
- Injection and input-validation failures in clinical data entry forms
- Weak session handling on shared clinical workstations
